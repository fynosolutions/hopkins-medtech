Diagnostic accuracy of rapid antigen tests in asymptomatic and presymptomatic close contacts of individuals with confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection: cross sectional study

BMJ (Clinical research ed.) · 2021
Paper authors: Schuit E, Veldhuijzen IK, Venekamp RP, van den Bijllaardt W, Pas SD, Lodder EB, Molenkamp R, GeurtsvanKessel CH, Velzing J, Huisman RC, Brouwer L, Boelsums TL, Sips GJ, Benschop KSM, Hooft L, van de Wijgert JHHM, van den Hof S, Moons KGM.
Research reading note · Based on the abstract · Editorial summary prepared for this HMC website preview
HMC / PERSPECTIVES
IVD

A study of close contacts illustrates why intended-use populations and testing time must be defined together.

What the paper explores

This cross-sectional study assessed rapid antigen testing in asymptomatic and presymptomatic close contacts of people with confirmed infection. Diagnostic sensitivity changed when the analysis considered subsequent symptoms or used a viral-load threshold as a proxy for infectiousness.

How to interpret the evidence

A viral-load threshold is a proxy, not a direct measurement of transmission. Findings from close contacts tested at a defined point after exposure do not establish accuracy for every screening use.

Questions for MedTech teams

Plan separate analyses for the intended clinical question. Detecting any infection and identifying a high viral-load subset are different evaluation targets.
